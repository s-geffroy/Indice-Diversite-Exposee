# What privacy costs an audit

!!! success "The regulated quantity is nearly free to protect"
    The share of the population below the floor — what the protocol prescribes publishing — does
    not move by a thousandth between $\varepsilon = 10$ and $\varepsilon = 0.1$. A 50-bin
    histogram over 58,549 user-days is out of the noise's reach.

!!! danger "The quantity it depends on is not"
    At $\varepsilon = 1$ — the usual reference value — attention severity goes from **0.88 to
    0.50**, and its standard error does not move. The result is wrong **with confidence**, and in
    the direction that makes burial look milder than it is.

!!! warning "And the real price is not the noise"
    It is the **contribution bound**, without which no *per-person* guarantee is possible. It
    shifts the share below the floor by **+0.6 to +2.5 points**, always upward: assiduous readers
    are more diverse than the rest, and dropping them makes the platform look less compliant than
    it is.

## A gap the repository had flagged against itself

The [Article 40 request](article-40.en.md) proves a regulator does not need the log: four
aggregate tables suffice, for 95 times fewer rows. It says nothing about their **harmlessness**,
and the [audit](limites.en.md) admitted as much without remedying it:

> A credible audit protocol must be aggregative and **differentially private** — this repository
> does not propose one.

An aggregate is not anonymous. The small-cell suppression threshold used until now offers **no
formal guarantee**: it makes the attack laborious, not impossible.

## 1. Two distinct costs

**Noise** protects against inference about one row of the log. Its effect depends on the
**density** of the published table, not on the importance of the quantity:

| Table | Cells | Median count | Survives down to |
|---|---|---|---|
| rank margin of displays | 9 | 56,000 | $\varepsilon = 0.03$ |
| index histogram per user-day | 50 | 1,100 | $\varepsilon = 0.1$ |
| **(item, rank) cells** | **451,100** | **1** | **nothing** |

99.7 % of the cells in table 3 carry fewer than five impressions. That is the only thing that
matters, and it explains everything else.

The **contribution bound** is what makes a *per-person* guarantee possible: at most $C$
observations are kept per reader. It is a design choice, not an artefact, and it is what shifts
the figures.

## 2. The regulated quantity holds

| Cap | Days kept | Bound's bias | $\varepsilon = 1$ | $\varepsilon = 0.1$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 26 % | **+0.025** | 0.213 | 0.213 |
| 3 | 63 % | +0.014 | 0.201 | 0.201 |
| 5 | 86 % | +0.006 | 0.194 | 0.194 |
| 8 (none) | 100 % | 0.000 | 0.188 | 0.188 |

*True share below 0.40: 0.188.*

Noise is invisible across the whole range. Bounding adds up to 2.5 points to a share of 18.8 % —
a 13 % relative excess, **always in the same direction**.

That is unexpected, and it falls on the regulator's side: under a per-user guarantee, one
**overstates** non-compliance. The error committed in protecting readers does not exonerate the
platform.

## 3. Severity does not hold

| $\varepsilon$ | noisy $\eta$ | standard error | $\eta$ after publication threshold | items kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.871 | 0.047 | 0.871 | 145 |
| 3 | 0.797 | 0.052 | **0.881** | 57 |
| **1** | **0.505** | 0.051 | 1.034 | 25 |
| 0.3 | 0.151 | 0.050 | 1.201 | 9 |

*Without noise: $\eta = 0.882 \pm 0.046$ over 143 items.*

Noise on counts acts as **measurement error**: it attenuates the estimate towards zero, and the
standard error does not say so. It is the failure mode this repository has met five times — a
plausible figure, a tight interval, a false conclusion.

**The publication threshold corrects the level, then overshoots it.** Keeping only cells whose
noisy count exceeds $\tau$ trades an attenuation bias for a **selection** bias: what survives are
the most exposed cells, hence top ranks and popular items. At $\varepsilon = 3$ the trade is
good; below, it turns bad. The remedy does not remove the error, it flips its sign.

## 4. The knot

The rank margin survives $\varepsilon = 0.03$, and on this log it lands within **0.7 %** of the
item-fixed-effects value — 0.876 against 0.882.

But we only know that because we hold the item-level table, the one no guarantee protects.

!!! danger "What publishes safely is what cannot be checked"
    On a platform where composition varied more with rank, the margin could be wrong **with
    nothing to show it**. The verifiable quantity and the publishable quantity are not the same,
    and no privacy budget reconciles them.

## 5. What this changes in the request

**One budget per quantity, published with it.** Budgets do not compose for free: two tables at
$\varepsilon$ each give a guarantee at $2\varepsilon$. A real request must add them up and say so.

**Table 3 must not be requested as it stands.** Three requests replace it:

1. the **rank margin** of displays, publishable at $\varepsilon = 0.1$ without damage;
2. the **item-fixed-effects estimate computed by the platform itself**, published as a noised
   scalar — a number has bounded sensitivity, a sparse table does not;
3. the **gap between the two**, the only available check of the first by the second.

**The contribution bound must be published with the result**, as the suppression threshold
already is. It is not neutral, and its direction is known.

## Reservations

The mechanism used is the simplest there is — Laplace noise on counts, a publication threshold
for the $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ guarantee. Mechanisms better suited to sparse tables exist and
would do better: this chapter **bounds the cost from above**, it does not claim to attain it.

The *per-user* guarantee is measured only on EB-NeRD, the repository's only log carrying a reader
identifier. On Baidu-ULTR only a **per-impression** guarantee is possible — weaker, and a
property of the dataset, not of the method.

Finally, the bounding bias is computed **in expectation** from the digest's load × index
cross-table; a real draw departs from it by a few thousandths.
