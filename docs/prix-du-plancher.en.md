# What a floor costs, on real feeds

!!! danger "The floor this repository proposes constrains almost nothing"
    Bringing the **whole** population above 0.40 requires replacing **one served item in 177** —
    0.57 % of the log. The median user-day needing treatment requires a **single substitution**.
    What 99.4 % of served items show stays unchanged.

!!! warning "And the shape of the regulated quantity invites treating the margin"
    The protocol prescribes the **share of the population below the threshold**, precisely so
    that a satisfactory mean cannot hide an enclosed minority. But a share is a threshold, and a
    threshold is met **at the margin**: you fix the readers *just below*, and the most enclosed
    stay where they are.

!!! failure "Above 0.50, a floor regulates volume, not diversity"
    At 0.60, **10.9 %** of user-days are out of reach whatever the platform does; at 0.80,
    **22.9 %**. These are not enclosed readers, they are **light** ones: five items cap out at
    $\log_2 5 / \log_2 26 = 0.49$. The only possible compliance is to serve them **more**.

---

## A price that assumes nothing

The repository has priced a diversity standard since the [baselines](lignes-de-base.en.md) — 8.2 %
to 18.9 % of engagement. That figure has an acknowledged flaw: it **assumes relevance known**, and
bears on simulated feeds.

The 58,549 user-days of [chapter 26](indice-mesure.en.md) allow the question to be asked without
assuming anything: **how many served items would have to be replaced** for a real population to
cross a floor? The unit is the least disputable there is.

## 1. The check that decides the rest

The cost is computed by a **greedy** procedure: each substitution removes an item from the
most-served section and adds one to the least-served. Nothing guarantees *a priori* that it is
optimal.

The repository's rule, applied: enumerate **every** possible redistribution over two hundred
compositions short enough to make it feasible. **200 out of 200 identical.** The price that
follows is therefore not a cautious bound, it is the price.

## 2. The price, level by level

| Floor | Below the threshold | Population that can be lifted | Items to change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.30 | 4.6 % | 4.6 % | **0.21 %** |
| **0.40** | 13.7 % | 13.7 % | **0.57 %** |
| 0.50 | 50.7 % | 46.0 % | **2.27 %** |
| 0.60 | 96.2 % | 85.3 % | **8.24 %** |
| 0.70 | 100 % | 84.9 % | **17.11 %** |

The price is **convex**: doubling the floor from 0.30 to 0.60 multiplies the cost by forty. A
regulator therefore has real room — and the level this repository has used so far sits at the
painless end of the curve.

But one column does not read like the others: **from 0.50 on, the population that can be lifted
above the threshold is smaller than the population below it.**

## 3. The ceiling nothing can lift

An entropy cannot exceed the logarithm of the number of items **served**. A reader who receives
five caps out at 0.49, and no editorial desk will lift them out.

| Floor | User-days out of reach | Items served (median) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.40 | 0.0 % | — |
| 0.50 | 4.7 % | 5 |
| 0.60 | 10.9 % | 6 |
| 0.70 | 15.1 % | 6 |
| 0.80 | 22.9 % | 8 |

This ceiling is not a limit of measurement: it is a limit of the **regulated object**. A high
floor counts as non-compliant readers whose only fault is reading little, and pushes the platform
to serve them more — exactly what a diversity standard has no reason to encourage.

## 4. What this changes

**The chosen level must rise, or the standard is decorative.** At 0.40 over twenty-six sections,
one item in 177 suffices. [Chapter 28](catalogue.en.md) showed the level means nothing without its
catalogue; this one shows that at the chosen level, it does not bite.

**The regulated quantity must be rethought.** The share below the threshold was chosen against
masking by the mean; it invites treating the margin. A quantity resisting both remains to be found
— a floor on a **low quantile** of the distribution, for instance, would force fixing the most
enclosed rather than the closest to the line.

**And a floor must account for volume served.** Two ways out, and one must be chosen: exempt short
sessions, or normalise the index by the **attainable** maximum rather than by $\log_2 k$. The
second changes the index's definition; the first adds one more threshold.

## Reservations

The cost is measured in **items exchanged**, not in engagement: EB-NeRD provides no relevance
model, and a substitution may be painless or costly. The 0.57 % figure therefore does not say
compliance is *easy*, only that it is **narrow in extent**.

The computation assumes **any section can be served to anyone**, which overstates ease: a
newspaper does not always have the article it would need, when it would need it.

Finally, all of this holds for this corpus's twenty-six-section catalogue. On a coarser catalogue,
the floor bites even less.
